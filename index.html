Parameterized Complexity and Vertex Cover

15-455 project: fixed-parameter algorithms, kernelization, and the 3k-vertex kernel theorem.

Start with section 1: Motivation

The plan

Project scope

This project continues the topic selected last week: parameterized complexity, with Vertex Cover as the running example. The target is approximately 160 minutes of material, organized as two 80-minute lectures. Lecture 1 will cover fixed-parameter algorithms and basic preprocessing; Lecture 2 will develop crown reductions and prove the linear kernel theorem. The material will be presented as lecture notes with definitions, proofs, static diagrams, and fully worked examples.

The intended audience is students with the mathematical background of a 15-455 student. The site will assume familiarity with graphs, asymptotic running time, and NP-hardness; it will introduce the matching concepts needed for the main proof.

Current stage: proposed outline. The explanations, proofs, and worked examples described below are planned content.

160-minute teaching plan

Lecture plan

Lecture 1 80 minutes

Parameters, branching, and a first kernel

  1. Motivation and Vertex Cover10 min
  2. FPT and the role of the parameter15 min
  3. Branching: algorithm, proof, and analysis25 min
  4. Safe reductions and a quadratic kernel30 min

Target outcome: prove the branching algorithm correct and derive the quadratic kernel bound.

Lecture 2 80 minutes

From matching structure to a linear kernel

  1. Matchings, crowns, and reduction safety30 min
  2. Crown finding and the 3k theorem35 min
  3. Worked example, reconstruction, and recap15 min

Target outcome: justify crown reduction and assemble the polynomial-time 3k-vertex kernel proof.

These are teaching-time estimates for the finished material, not reading times for this outline. References and optional further directions are outside the 160 minutes.
