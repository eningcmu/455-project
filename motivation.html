Section 01 of 07

Why parameterized complexity?

Theory notes

NP-completeness and what it leaves open

NP-completeness is a worst-case classification: unless P=NP, an NP-complete problem has no polynomial-time algorithm for all inputs. But that label does not tell us whether instances with useful structure can still be solved efficiently.

Parameterized complexity gives a finer view. It asks whether the expensive part of an algorithm can be confined to a function of k, while the dependence on the rest of the input remains polynomial.

Vertex Cover review

Recall the parameterized decision problem: given a graph G=(V,E) and a nonnegative integer k, decide whether G has a vertex cover of size at most k.

Definition. (G,k) is a yes-instance exactly when there is a set C⊆V such that |C|≤k and, for every edge uv∈E, at least one of u and v belongs to C. Such a set is a vertex cover.

Why use a parameter?

Vertex Cover is NP-complete when k is part of the input. Brute force checks all subsets of at most k vertices, taking roughly nk time. For a fixed k this is polynomial, but its exponent grows with k; it does not give one polynomial exponent that works for every parameter value. The parameterized viewpoint asks whether small-budget instances can be solved efficiently even when the graph is large.
